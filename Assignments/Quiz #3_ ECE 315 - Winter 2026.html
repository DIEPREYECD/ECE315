Your Answer:

To solve this issue we can use simple multiplexing idea of all the peripherals sharing the SCK, SDI, and SDO pins and then we use the 4 GPIO pins as a way of selecting which peripheral(this allows us to potentially be able to support up to 16 different peripherals) is currently active preventing interference or overlap between the peripherals. 

 

So the 4 GPIO pins (GP10-GP13) connected to a 4-16 demultiplexer can select the peripherals, other pins can be assigned as GP18=SCK, GP19=MOSI, GP16=MISO.

An SPI ADC:

  • 16-bit frame
  • SPI clock = 8 MHz
  • CS overhead = 1 µs per transaction

(a) Compute transaction time per sample.
(b) Determine maximum sample rate.
(c) If RTOS adds 10 µs scheduling jitter, determine effective max sample rate.

Show your work and state your assumptions.

Your Answer:

SPI ADC Timing Analysis

Given:

  • ADC frame size = 16 bits
  • SPI clock = 8 MHz
  • Chip-select (CS) overhead = 1 µs per transaction
  • RTOS scheduling jitter = 10 µs

My assumptions:

  • One ADC sample is transferred in exactly one 16-bit SPI transaction.
  • SPI transfers one bit per clock cycle.
  • The 1 µs CS overhead occurs once per sample transaction.
  • The 10 µs RTOS scheduling jitter is added to the total sample interval in the worst case.

(a) Compute transaction time per sample

First, compute the SPI clock period:

Tbit = 1 / fSPI = 1 / (8 × 106) s

Tbit = 0.125 µs

Now compute the time to transfer 16 bits:

TSPI = 16 × 0.125 µs = 2 µs

Add the chip-select overhead:

Tsample = TSPI + TCS

Tsample = 2 µs + 1 µs = 3 µs

Answer (a): The transaction time per sample is 3 µs.

(b) Determine maximum sample rate

The maximum sample rate is the inverse of the sample time:

fmax = 1 / Tsample = 1 / (3 × 10-6)

fmax = 333,333.3 samples/s

fmax ≈ 333 kS/s

Answer (b): The maximum sample rate is 333,333 samples/s (about 333 kS/s).

(c) Effective maximum sample rate with 10 µs RTOS scheduling jitter

If the RTOS adds 10 µs of scheduling jitter, then the effective sample interval becomes:

Teffective = Tsample + Tjitter

Teffective = 3 µs + 10 µs = 13 µs

Now compute the effective maximum sample rate:

feffective = 1 / Teffective = 1 / (13 × 10-6)

feffective = 76,923.1 samples/s

feffective ≈ 76.9 kS/s

Answer (c): With RTOS scheduling jitter, the effective maximum sample rate is 76,923 samples/s (about 76.9 kS/s).

Final Answers

  • (a) Transaction time per sample = 3 µs
  • (b) Maximum sample rate = 333,333 samples/s (about 333 kS/s)
  • (c) Effective maximum sample rate with 10 µs jitter = 76,923 samples/s (about 76.9 kS/s)

(a) The time per 16-bit ADC sample is 16 bits / 8 Mbps = 16 / 8,000,000 = 2 µs

(b) Assuming the chip select overhead is constant, then 2 + 1 = 3 µs are needed per sample and the maximum sample rate will be S = 1 sample / 3 µs = 333,333.333 samples per second or 333.33 kSps

(c ) The question is not well enough specified to know how much the scheduling jitter affects the maximum rate. For example, if the scheduler has time slices of 30 ms, we incur a 10 µs delay every 30 ms, then 30 ms / 3 µs / sample = 30,000 / 3 = 10,000 samples. Every 10,000 samples we would add 10 µs. That is, 10,000 samples would take 30,000 µs + 10 µs = 30,010 µs, and thus 10,000 samples / 30,010 µs = 333.22 kSps.

In general we can say that for time slices of N s, the time to get  M samples = N s / 3 µs is M samples * 3 µs / sample + 10 µs. The data rate is therefore R =  ((N s * 1000000 µs / s) / 3 µs / (N s * 1000000 µs / s  + 10 µs)

For N = 0.010 s, R = 0.010 * 1000000 / 3 / (0.010 * 1000000 + 10) = 0.333000 µs/sample = 333.00 kSps
