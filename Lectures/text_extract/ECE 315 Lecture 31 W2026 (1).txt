Open–loop Control
• In open-loop control, there is no knowledge of the motor state (position, speed,
  power, …) that is returned (aka, “fed back”) to the controller
• Sometimes it doesn’t really matter…
• If needed, it is estimated using some sort of model, e.g., manually step the motor
  to a starting position, then count the steps made and in which direction
   • This might be “good enough”

Closed–loop Control
• In closed-loop control, the motor state (position, speed, power, …) is directly
  measured and returned to the controller to aid in motor control
• The control algorithm is generally digital, which means that analogue signals
  from the position or other sensor must be converted.
• The control algorithm computes the difference between the desired position and
  the actual position, then determines the best next step command(s)

Servos
• A servo motor is an electromechanical actuator
  designed for precise control of position,
  velocity, or torque using closed-loop feedback.
   • Used in radio-controlled aircraft to control the flight surfaces: rudder (yaw control), elevator
     (pitch control), and ailerons (roll control).
• Closed-loop control: A feedback device (typically a potentiometer or encoder)
  continuously measures the output and compares it to a commanded setpoint.
• Integrated or Component system: Usually consists of a motor (DC, AC, or
  brushless), a gear train, a feedback sensor, and a control circuit.
• Commanded motion: Moves to and holds a specific position or follows a trajectory,
  correcting errors automatically.
• High accuracy & repeatability: Well-suited for applications requiring precise motion

Servos cont’d
• A widely used control interface for servos uses an analog signaling
  method called pulse width modulation (PWM)
  • Many microcontrollers have built-in PWM peripherals
     • Zynq-7000 has 6 timers that can generate PWM signals at MIO pins
     • RP2040 has up to 16 controllable PWM outputs
• A train of positive pulses at a fixed rate, e.g., at 50 Hz
• Pulses are fixed amplitude (e.g., 3 to 5 V)
• Width is controlled to be within some range about a neutral width
  (e.g., 1.5 ms +/- 0.5 ms).
• The pulse width determines the angle of a rotor

Brushed DC Motor
• DC motors where current is provided to
  the rotor coil via contacts on a
  commutator are known as “brushed”
• The motor is constructed so that when
  current flows a field opposing the stator
  (permanent) magnetic field is induced
  which creates a torque that turns the
  rotor
• The split commutator ensures the
  current is reversed every 180° so that
  the rotation is continued

 Brushless DC Motor

 • A brushless DC motor eliminates the need
   for a brushed commutator by inverting the
   motor configuration
 • Instead of the rotor having two or more
   coiled arms, the stator has the coils
 • The rotor is comprised of two or more
   permanent magnets

 Mechanical Switches/Relays
 • A mechanical relay uses a magnetic coil
   to change the state of a mechanical
   electrical switch
 • Typically, the relay has a configuration of
   contacts to a common conductor that are
   either normally-open or normally-closed
 • This provides flexibility for the logic of the
   switch
 • The coil circuit is physically (electrically)
   isolated from the switching circuit
     • Means that the switching circuit can be high-
       voltage and/or high-current

Solenoid

• An electro-mechanical device that uses an energized coil to create a
  magnetic field, producing linear motion of a movable plunger
• It converts electrical energy into short-stroke mechanical force for
  actuation, switching, or control functions

Summary
• Motion control systems may operate in open-loop (no feedback) or closed-loop
  (sensor feedback) configurations, with trade-offs in complexity, accuracy, and
  robustness
• Rotary shaft encoders (optical, magnetic, magneto-resistive) provide position
  information that enables precise closed-loop motor control
• Servo motors integrate motors, feedback, and control electronics to achieve
  accurate position control, commonly using PWM interfaces
• Brushed and brushless DC motors are combined with H-bridge circuits to control
  direction and speed via PWM
• Other electro-mechanical actuators—including linear actuators, solenoids, and
  mechanical or solid-state relays—extend embedded systems beyond rotary
  motion and enable controlled mechanical and electrical switching
