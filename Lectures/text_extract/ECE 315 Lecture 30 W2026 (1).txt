Actuators, Relays, Servos and Stepper Motors
• Microcomputers are often required to control the movement of mechanical
  devices or to set other physical parameters.
• An actuator is a device that is used by a microcomputer to control an external
  physical quantity, such as position.
• A relay is an electrical switch that is closed and opened by driving or not driving
  current through a magnetic coil.
   • Normally open (n.o.) relay: close the switch by energizing
   • Normally closed (n.c.) relay: open the switch by energizing
• A servo motor is an actuator that is used to cause mechanical rotation within
  some fixed range of angles (<360 deg.).
• A stepper motor is an actuator whose rotational position (360 deg.) and speed
  can be controlled by digital signals.

Stepper Motors
• An electromechanical actuator that converts a sequence of electrical
  control pulses into discrete, incremental angular motion
  • Each input pulse causes the motor shaft to rotate by a fixed step angle,
    allowing precise control of position and speed without requiring feedback
Key characteristics
  • Discrete motion: Rotation occurs in fixed steps (e.g., 1.8° per step → 200
    steps/revolution).
  • Open-loop control: Position is determined by counting steps; no encoder is
    required in basic operation.
  • High holding torque: Can maintain position when energized.
  • Repeatable positioning: Accuracy depends on step angle and load conditions.

Stepper Motor Operation –
Simplified
• A rotating rotor contains a permanent
  magnet with fixed north (N) and
  south (S) poles
• A stationary stator is energized with
  current to produce an electro-magnet
  • The location of the N and S poles of the electromagnet is determined by the
    current direction through the 1 or more winding(s)
• The rotor experiences a torque that acts to bring the rotor into
  alignment with the stator (the N and S poles attract one another)
  • The torque disappears when the stator is fully aligned with the rotor

 Hybrid Stepper Motor

 • A hybrid stepper has a multi-pole stator
   and a two-part rotor
 • The rotor comprises two physically identical
   multi-tooth permanent magnets, offset by a
   half tooth
 • They are magnetized along the shaft axis,
   one north the other south
 • The stator coils are wound on alternating
   poles with corresponding teeth

Hybrid Stepper Motor cont’d
• The 48 teeth on one rotor disk are offset
  by half a tooth with respect to the other
  disk
• Due to the offset, the rotor effectively has
  96 interleaved poles of opposite polarity
• This allows for a step of 1/96th of a
  revolution by reversing the phase of a
  winding
• With two stator phases, there are 2x96
  steps per revolution
• This means that the step angle is
  360°/192 = 1.875°

 Sophisticated Motor Drivers – TMC2209
• The chip provides two interfaces
  • STEP/DIR; A CPU (µC) generates step & direction signals synchronized to
    additional motors and other components within the system. The TMC2209
    operates the motor as commanded by the configuration pins & STEP/DIR signals
  • UART; uni- or bi-directional
    The µC sends commands to the controller via the UART interface; supports
     • Detailed diagnostics and thermal management
     • Passive braking and freewheeling for flexible, lowest power stop modes
     • More options for microstep resolution setting (fullstep to 256 microstep)
     • Software controlled motor current setting and more chopper options
     • Use StallGuard for sensorless homing and CoolStep for adaptive motor current and
       cool motor

Summary
• Embedded systems can initiate and control physical motion using actuators such as relays,
  servos, DC motors, and stepper motors
• Stepper motors provide precise, repeatable positioning by converting digital pulse sequences into
  discrete angular steps (open-loop control)
• Full-step and half-step drive modes determine step resolution and smoothness through controlled
  coil energization sequences
• Special construction of stepper stator and rotors support small angle steps
    • Typically, a single step is 1.8°. Micro-stepping supports even finer steps
• H-bridge circuits enable bidirectional current flow in motor windings and are essential for
  microcontroller-based motor control
• Modern driver ICs (e.g., TMC2209) integrate current regulation, microstepping, diagnostics, &
  communication interfaces (STEP/DIR, UART) to simplify & enhance motion control system design
