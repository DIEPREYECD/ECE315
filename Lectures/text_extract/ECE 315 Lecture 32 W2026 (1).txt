Learning Objectives
• Explain the operation of an H-bridge circuit and how it enables bidirectional
  control and speed control (via PWM) of DC motors
• Implement stepper motor control using the L298N, including generating
  appropriate coil activation sequences for full-step and half-step operation
• Explain how GPIO control and timing parameters affect stepper motor behavior,

  complexity, features (e.g., microstepping), and application use cases

The Half- and Full-step sequences are defined. They are the pattern of inputs that
energize the A and B coils separately and together to cause a half or full step,
respectively.

Some convenient functions to manage multiple steps in one direction are defined and
the GPIIO initialized.

  L298N Controller




                                                                                     11




stepper_set_outputs creates a GPIO pin mask based on the input sequence
aka pattern. It then uses synchronous IO commands that are implicit in the two
gpio_ commands to turn off briefly all puns and then turn on all those that are
needed in this step.

We want to avoid turning them on or off individually for number of practical reasons,
probably the most important being that we don’t want to overlap signals when they
should not be, or create glitches when they should be.

The last function, initialize_stepper, ensures that the driver starts in the
right place. That is, assuming the motor is positioned in its starting rotation, which we
assume represents 0 degrees.

Since we have no encoder or other way to determine position, this is in effect and
open-loop initialization.
